# Changes to the TVTMS data

<!-- cspell:ignore Standardisation -->

The mapping tables here are generated from STEPBible's TVTMS (Translators Versification Traditions with Methodology for Standardisation), by STEP Bible (https://www.STEPBible.org), based on work at Tyndale House Cambridge, CC BY 4.0. Source: https://github.com/STEPBible/STEPBible-Data at commit `be4191f0082aeedddb0eff0c02c966292b2bd641`, file `Versification/TVTMS - … STEPBible.org CC BY.txt`.

They are generated by `tools/source-conversion` (`pnpm tvtms`); `import-report.txt` lists every row left out. No mapping in TVTMS has been corrected. What we did to the data:

1. **Reformatted.** Only the expanded section is used. Each tradition's rows (verse → KJV standard verse) are composed with the canonical scheme's rows, giving pairs of OSIS references, [scheme verse, canonical verse]. Pairs with the same number on both sides are left out.
2. **Canonical scheme.** The canonical scheme is the Hebrew tradition for the Old Testament and the Greek tradition for the additional books and the New Testament. The Greek tradition's placement of the additions to Daniel and Psalm 151 inside Daniel and Psalms isn't used for the canonical scheme; those keep their own books.
3. **Alternatives.** Where a tradition has alternative mappings told apart by TVTMS's tests (e.g. Greek editions of Sirach 20), the first alternative in the file is used and the others are left out. The tests themselves aren't evaluated.
4. **Rows left out:**
   - `Concatenation` rows (summaries of the rows after them), `AllBibles` rows and `IfEmpty verse` rows (an empty slot kept where a verse is missing).
   - Subverses beyond the ninth (`!j`, `!aa` …), mostly in the Greek of Exodus 36–39, 1 Kings, Esther and 2 Esdras 7. Our references hold at most nine parts of a verse.
   - The additions to Esther as a separate book (`Ade`), and `4Es`. Neither is in our book registry.
