# @scripturetips/web

The web app: Next.js (App Router), server-rendered, with next-intl for every user-facing string. So far: the landing page, the reader view, the credits page, sign-in with the account page, and the compilation editor. It reads scripture from the API (`API_URL`).

- **Reader view:** `/read/{translation}/{book}/{chapter}[/{verses}]` (domain-model §1.6), e.g. `/read/leb/genesis/2/18-25`, `/read/kjv/psalms/23`; several passages as `/read/{translation}/passage/joel.2.28,joel.3.1`. A book outside the reader's canon is labelled "Additional writings"; with no canon chosen, books the traditions disagree on are labelled as such (`@scripturetips/canon` `standing`). **Book list:** `/read/{translation}` lists the translation's books in the reader's canon order (`src/reader/books.ts`), each opening to its chapters; books printed inside another (Greek Esther) are listed with it. Each passage ends with previous and next chapter links in the same order. The search form posts to `/read?t=&ref=`, which reads the reference through the API and redirects to the canonical path.
- **Publications** (`src/publications/`, `src/app/p/`): `/p/{handle}/{slug}` lists a published compilation's books and `/p/{handle}/{slug}/{book}/{chapter}[/{verses}]` reads it, at the latest version (public only) or pinned by a version ID at the end of the path (`splitVersion`); readable signed out, with the version and its date, a link to a newer version or to this version's permanent link, and the credit lines (`creditLines`, shared with `/read`). Pages declare the pinned path canonical, and unlisted ones are `noindex`. The compilation editor's *Publish* section runs the checks on request (they read every verse, so not on page load; `PublishChecks`), then asks for consent and publishes unlisted or public, with a change note, and lists the published versions.
- **Attributions** (`src/reader/attributions.tsx`): where a license asks (licenses.md §8, `attribution`), the reader places the designation after each run of verses from that source ("(LEB)", linked) and shows the credit statement with its links: a publication's as published, a translation's from its license.
- **Accounts** (`src/account/`): `/sign-in` asks for an email address and the ALTCHA bot check, then the emailed code; `/account` sets the handle, display name and canon profile, and signs out. Requests to `/api/auth/*` and `/api/account*` are proxied to the API (`next.config.ts`), so the session cookie belongs to the site the user sees. `currentAccount()` reads the account in server components; the header shows "Sign in" or the user's handle. Sign-in emails land in Mailpit: http://127.0.0.1:8025.
- **Compilations** (`src/compilations/`): `/compilations` lists the user's compilations as cards (the logo as a square the card's height, then the title over its composition bar with each source's abbreviation; the whole card opens the editor, which is also the compilation's read-only view) and starts one from a modal opened by *+ New compilation* (a handle is needed first); there is no link to a compilation's `/read/@handle/slug` reader, and an address with nothing at it, such as an unpublished compilation's `/p/{handle}/{slug}`, shows `src/app/not-found.tsx`; `/compilations/{slug}` is the editor, full width on large screens. At the top, on the left: the logo and title, then the abbreviation (grey) under them, each changed in place (`InPlaceField`, `PATCH /api/compilations/{slug}`; a new abbreviation moves it to its new path), and under them a bar of where its verses come from, split by source (`CompositionChart`, loaded after the page from `GET /api/compilations/{slug}/composition`, which reads every verse), with *Publish*, *Build PDF* and a red *Delete* under the bar, each opening a modal dialog (`ModalButton`, a native `<dialog>`): publishing with its checks, consent and versions; the PDF builds; and deleting, which needs "delete this compilation" typed first (`DeleteCompilationForm`). On the right, the books by section as tabs (`BookTabs`); each book name opens it at chapter 1 in the reading area, followed by its menu (its icon shows on hover or focus; Tab goes book, its menu, next book): *Change the book translation* gives the whole book a new source after a warning, throwing away every narrower choice within it (`PUT /api/compilations/{slug}/books/{book}/source`). The menu is placed against the window so the book columns don't re-balance around it. The bar counts again whenever the compilation's choices change (a fingerprint of them, passed from the page), keeping the last result with a spinner, and shows a skeleton before the first count. Under all that, the reading area (`ReadingArea`) shows one chapter, kept in the address (`?book=Gen&chapter=1&mode=read`), under the mode buttons on a light blue header bar: previous and next at its ends, the book and chapter in the middle, where hovering or focusing the chapter number lists every chapter (CSS only, so it works before hydration). It has three modes: *Read* (the text as printed, in columns), *Compare* (`CompareGrid`: a table with one row per verse of the compilation and translations beside it (three to start; a column's + adds one after it, up to ten, and its × removes it; past three the table is clipped at the page's edge and slides sideways by a scrollbar held under the sticky head, by sideways wheel or trackpad movement, or by keyboard focus, the compilation's column held at the left, while the page itself scrolls down), lined up by canonical verse whatever their numbering, from `GET /api/compilations/{slug}/compare?ref=&with=`; each column's translation is chosen in its header and saved with the compilation, `PUT /api/compilations/{slug}/compare-with`), and *Edit* (the same, where a click on a verse takes it into the compilation and Shift-click picks the first and last of a run, taken when Shift is let go; the compilation's column header has the same menu as a book's, giving the whole chapter a new translation after a warning, `PUT /api/compilations/{slug}/chapters/{Gen.1}/source`, and Escape clears the picks; `POST /api/compilations/{slug}/verses/batch`). `/compilations/{slug}/verse?ref=` is the verse editor: the verse as the compilation has it, every translation's wording with *Use this*, a form to correct or adapt a translation's wording, and *Write your own rendering* (`OwnRenderingForm`: typing is sent to the authorship journal in bursts after a pause; pastes, undo and redo as their own events), with the declaration and, for a saved rendering, its evidence (record intact or not, declaration, similarity to each translation, the evidence download, and declaring a pasted one), and the verse's translator's notes (`NotesEditor`: reword, remove or restore a source's note, add, move or remove an own one); changing a note on a verse taken unchanged makes it a correction. Dates are held in UTC and shown in the browser's time zone (`LocalDate`, `src/dates.tsx`). The reader marks corrected verses †, adapted ones ‡, own renderings § and pasted ones *, and credits who changed which translation or wrote their own; a note adapted from a translation, or added in the compilation, says so in the notes list. Only translations whose license allows it are offered (`@scripturetips/licenses`). A compilation is read at `/read/@{handle}/{slug}/…` like a translation (`readerTarget`, `ownsCompilation` in `src/reader/paths.ts`), with a credit line naming its sources; `/api/compilations*` is proxied with the session cookie, and compilation requests are never cached, except a compilation's book list, cached under its base and rules (`books(id, version)`), which a change to either asks again. The previous and next chapter links are prefetched (in a production build). Someone else's compilation is not found.
- **Forms handled by script** keep their submit button disabled until the page has hydrated (`useHydrated`, `src/hydrated.ts`), so an early click can't submit natively and put the input in the URL. A controlled field (the verse editor's wording, the own rendering) is read-only until then, or text typed early gets mixed with the original or goes unrecorded.
- **The bot check needs a secure context** (HTTPS or `localhost`), since it uses Web Crypto. So the dev stack serves the web app over HTTPS, with a self-signed certificate `./dev` makes in `.dev-certs/` (not in git) for localhost and the machine's network addresses: a phone or tablet on `./dev up --lan` can sign in after accepting the browser's warning once.
- **Rendering** (`src/reader/`): `layout.ts` lays verses out as printed (paragraphs and poetry lines running across verses, headings, verse numbers where each verse's text begins, verse 0 as a title without a number) and letters note marks in reading order, nested ones included. `Passage.tsx` draws it: idiomatic phrases in lower corner brackets, translator's notes listed below with links both ways.
- **UI strings** live in `messages/en-NZ.json` (New Zealand English). Errors from the API arrive as codes and are worded there.
- **Branding:** docs/branding.md. Assets in `public/brand/` (their rights: docs/license-evidence/ and the credits page). The artwork is the fixed page background; content sits on a plain panel over it.
- **Running it:** `./dev up` starts it with the API and database, with hot reload, at https://127.0.0.1:3000. `./dev up --lan` serves the web app to the local network too (to try it on a phone or tablet); `./dev up` again goes back to localhost only. Agents always use `./dev up --lan`, since the project owner tests on a tablet.
- **Tests:** unit tests (`*.test.ts`, in `pnpm verify`), and Playwright end-to-end tests in `e2e/` against the running stack: `./dev up`, then `./toolbox sh -c 'cd apps/web && npx playwright test'`. The browser opens the app as `https://localhost:3000`, mapped to the `web` container, accepting the self-signed certificate; the sign-in tests read the code from Mailpit. Shared steps (signing up, the bot check, screenshots, a separate client address per test) are in `e2e/support.ts`. They save desktop and phone screenshots to `test-results/screens/`; look at them for any UI change (definition of done). They aren't in `pnpm verify` yet, since they need the stack running.

## UI conventions

- A button that adds an item to a list sits at the list's bottom left, centered on a phone (`.list-add`). A form's submit button sits at the form's bottom right (`.account-form` buttons, `align-self: flex-end`), with its other buttons before it. In a modal the form fills it and its button is centered; two buttons go in a `.actions` row, centered with space around them and the usual gap. See vision.md §8.

## Study mode (vision §3.2)

A **Study** button beside the modes (compare and edit; `?study=1`, kept through chapters and modes by `readingHref`) marks the words that can be studied. `ReadingArea` collects the Strong's numbers on the chapter (`study/units.ts`), asks the API once what each is (`POST /api/study/words`, `study/api.ts`), and wraps the compare view in `StudyProvider`; `VerseText` reads it and `ShownText` draws each tagged `w` word as `.study-word` with `data-category` (person, place, name, conjunction, verb, noun, other) and a title (the Hebrew or Greek word, its number, its gloss). A run of adjacent words sharing one number (the idiom "freely eat") is wrapped in `.c-study-unit`, dashed. **Paint only:** backgrounds, an inset edge and an outline, never padding, a border or a font change, so turning it on moves nothing (tested); the legend is a popover over the page for the same reason. Only the KJV family is marked (`STUDY_SOURCES`): other tagged translations tag the wrong words often (about one in four link to the exact original word, against nine in ten for the KJV). The colors are CSS variables (`--study-*`). Clicking a word does nothing yet.

## Compilation editor redesign (in progress)

The owner is redesigning the editor screen by screen size: desktop first (1920 × 1080), then phone, then the sizes between. Done so far: the full-width layout, the heading, the composition bar, the book tabs with their menus, and the reading area with its three modes. The list of layered choices and the form to add one, the single-verse list and its form, and Sources by book are gone from the editor. The API still has them all; the end-to-end tests set rules up through it, and a verse is opened at its editor's address. Still to refine when this is revisited:

- **Heading focus:** editing the abbreviation or title shows only by the caret (no border or focus ring, the owner's choice). Someone using the keyboard can't see which field has focus until the caret blinks; a faint underline on focus would fix it.
- **Reading area on phones:** not styled yet; the owner has his own plans for it. Until then the compare and edit table just scrolls sideways.
- **Before hydration:** a change to a column's translation made in the first moment after the page loads is lost (the end-to-end tests wait for the network to go quiet first).
- **Heading on phones:** the floating hint and error box covers part of the title while it's open, and a long title wraps below the abbreviation.

Run the unit tests: `./toolbox pnpm --filter @scripturetips/web test`
